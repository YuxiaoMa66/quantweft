**P5-01 — Hedge units, hand calculation.** Training regression $\log TR_y=\alpha+\beta\log TR_x+e$ gives $\beta=1.2$. Signal-close prices are $P_y=80$ and $P_x=50$. For this simplified share-sizing exercise, assume no split or dividend between the signal close and entry, and enter at those prices; do not separately model overnight price changes or execution costs. Take a long-spread position with target gross exposure USD 1,000,000. Using $w_y=1/(1+|\beta|)$ and $w_x=-\beta/(1+|\beta|)$, calculate dollar exposures, share quantities truncated toward zero, absolute and signed share ratios, and net dollar exposure. Why is the share ratio different from $\beta$? Does it still equal $|\beta|P_y/P_x$ exactly after rounding? In a level regression $P_y=a+bP_x+e$, what are the units of $b$?

**P5-02 — Corporate actions, hand calculation.** This is a corporate-action scenario independent of the P5-01 entry: before the events, the account already holds 5,681 shares of $y$ and is short 10,909 shares of $x$, with preceding closes of 80 and 50. At the next opening, $y$ splits 2:1 and $x$ goes ex-dividend by USD 0.30 per share. State both share and cash changes. Then apply $TR_t/TR_{t-1}=r_t(C_t+D_t)/C_{t-1}$: $y$ previously closed at 80 and closes post-split at 40.6; $x$ previously closed at 50 and closes ex-dividend at 49.9. What happens if vendor-adjusted prices are also used for execution?

**P5-03 — Financing and borrow, hand calculation.** Return to the original P5-01 entry, not the post-split/dividend P5-02 account. Initial cash is USD 1,000,000 with no holdings; ignore entry fees. This constructed cash model allows all short-sale proceeds to earn interest. Initial NAV is therefore also USD 1,000,000; gross target exposure alone would not establish it. Find post-entry cash. Friday 16:00 to Monday 09:30 lasts 65.5 hours in UTC or over a weekend without a DST change. Assume DFF 5.33% is known at interval start; this is a problem input, not a new historical rate check. The cash credit rate is DFF minus 25 bp; annual borrow is 25 bp; value the short at the available Friday close of USD 50. Under ACT/360, calculate days, interest, borrow charge, and ending cash.

**P5-04 — IS across partial fills on two days, hand calculation.** An exit sells $Q=-10{,}000$ shares. The decision price, in post-event signal-close units, is 41.00. Day 1 opens at 40.80 and fills only 6,000 shares, with a 2 bp half-spread and 5 bp impact. Day 2 opens at 40.50 and fills the remaining 4,000, with the same 2 bp half-spread and 4 bp impact. Commission is USD 0.005 per share. Use Chapter 36’s single-arrival convention to calculate delay, execution, fees, and total IS; cross-check the direct-dollar method. Examine this specific error: retain the original full-$Q$ first-arrival delay, reanchor day-2 execution to its opening price, and add $F_2$ times “day-2 open minus decision price” as delay. How much is overcounted? If first-day delay is allocated only to $F_1$, is reanchoring necessarily wrong?
